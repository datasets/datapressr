# Outline: Software's Slump Landed on the Youngest

*The skeleton. The prose is a rendering of this; if the prose drifts from the argument here, the prose is wrong.*

*Question (owner, bead `datapressr-t5s`): "What has happened to hiring for junior software developers?" Open mode: sources found and snapshotted for this story into [`junior-dev-hiring-src/`](junior-dev-hiring-src/PROVENANCE.md). As of 10 October 2026; the latest observations are Indeed postings for 2 October 2026, ADP payroll employment for August 2026 and the Current Population Survey (CPS) for September 2026.*

*Revision 3, after outline review rounds 1 and 2 (recorded at the end).*

## The argument, in one sentence

In one large payroll provider's records, US employment of software developers aged 22 to 25 has fallen by a fifth since November 2022 while every group over 30 grew, so the software jobs slump that began in 2022 has fallen hardest on the youngest, though the public Census survey is too small to confirm the age split.

## What this story is about

- **About:** what happened to the youngest software developers in the US compared with experienced ones, set against the slump in software job ads that preceded it. Age (22–25) is the proxy for junior; employment (a stock) is the proxy for hiring.
- **Not about:** proving a cause. These are counts of jobs and job ads. They show *when*, *where* and *for whom*, not *why*. Each explanation is attributed to a named source and marked as not tested. The data can exclude only two narrow things: that AI tools explain the part of the postings fall that happened before ChatGPT existed, and that the US had a software-specific extra drop that other countries did not.
- **Not about** the wrangling, which gets one short paragraph.

## Argument, in order

1. **The chart, first (chart 1).** Stanford Digital Economy Lab's index of US software-developer employment by age, from ADP payroll records, September 2021 to August 2026, November 2022 = 100. In August 2026: ages 22–25 at **80.5**; 26–30 at **93.9**; 31–34 at **105.5**; 35–40 at **111.7**; 41–49 at **119.5**; 50+ at **107.9**. The difference: 22–25 down a fifth (−19.5%), 41–49 up a fifth (+19.5%), a gap of **39 points**. The two youngest bands are below November 2022; the youngest by far the most. 22–25 peaked at **101.6** in September 2022.
   - *Weakening, stated:* November 2022 sits next to the 22–25 peak, so it flatters the fall. Measured from the series start (September 2021), 22–25 is down 8.7% (88.2 → 80.5) and 41–49 up 31% (91.2 → 119.5); 22–25 is still the only band below its September 2021 level. Both on chart 1 (start values labelled).
2. **What these numbers are.**
   - *Chart 1:* employment, not hiring: the number on payrolls at ADP client firms in the software developer occupation, by age, each series rescaled to 100 in November 2022 (the authors' choice; the month ChatGPT launched). Employment of the youngest is the closest proxy for entry-level hiring this data offers; it is a stock, so it also moves with quits, lay-offs and people ageing out of the band. Processed by Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen ("Canaries in the Coal Mine?", latest version August 2026); the payroll records are not public, the index is a public download (vintage 17 September 2026).
   - *Chart 2 and 3:* Indeed Hiring Lab's job postings index: software development postings and all postings on Indeed, seasonally adjusted, 7-day average, 1 February 2020 = 100. Ads, not hires, and not split by seniority in the open data. Charts use one reading a week.
3. **What it says.**
   - **Ads fell first, from early 2022 (chart 2).** US software postings peaked at **233.4** in the week of 26 February 2022 (the weekly series), fell to **61.1** on 17 May 2025, and stood at **78.3** on 2 October 2026: about a fifth below February 2020 (−22%), while all postings were at **103.8**, 4% above it. From the 2022 peak, software postings are down two-thirds (−66%), all postings a third (−36%).
   - **Timing against ChatGPT, both halves.** On 26 November 2022, four days before ChatGPT launched, software postings were at **143.2**: 90 of the 172 points of peak-to-low fall (52%) had already happened, so AI tools cannot explain that half. But software postings were then still 43% above February 2020; the whole fall below the pre-pandemic level came after the launch. Much of the first half is the unwinding of a doubling.
   - **Every country tracked shows it (chart 3).** From each series' peak (its highest weekly reading since January 2022; all in 2022 except French all postings, March 2023) to 2 October 2026, software postings fell **61% to 71%** in all six (US −66%, UK −66%, Canada −66%, Germany −71%, France −62%, Australia −61%), against falls of **33% to 60%** in all postings (US −36%, UK −60%, Canada −42%, Germany −40%, France −52%, Australia −33%). Software fell further than the market in every one. The UK is the exception in degree: its whole market fell, so its software-specific gap is small (−66% vs −60%).
   - **The young have not shared in the partial recovery.** US software postings are up from 61.1 to 78.3 since May 2025. The 22–25 employment index was **79.3** in April 2025 and **80.5** in August 2026: flat. (Every age series steps up about 2–4% between April and May 2025 in the ADP data, so May 2025 is not used as a base.) Indeed's researchers say 71% of the May 2025–May 2026 increase in software postings was for senior roles, and that in Q1 2026 senior roles were 69.3% of software development postings and entry-level ones 4.5% (Gallacher, 8 July 2026; Aidala and Puri, 23 July 2026; attributed, not in the open data). For someone entering now, that 4.5% is the plainest number.
   - Graduate outcome, attributed: in 2024, recent graduates (22–27) in computer science had an unemployment rate of **7.0%** and in computer engineering **7.8%**, against **4.2%** for all majors (New York Fed, from the American Community Survey). Cross-section only; the New York Fed publishes no trend by major.
4. **The wrinkle: the public survey cannot confirm the age split (chart 4).** The CPS, the Census Bureau's public household survey, gives the share of employed US software developers aged 22–25 as **8.2%, 8.1%, 8.5%, 7.9%, 9.1% and 7.1%** in the twelve months to September 2021–2026 (the last window has 11 months: October 2025 was not collected). The latest is the lowest, consistent with chart 1, but 2025's was the highest, and year-to-year swings are as large as the change in question. In level, the CPS shows about **159,000, 166,000, 179,000, 163,000, 201,000 and 174,000** employed developers aged 22–25 in those windows: no fall, while the all-ages total rose from about **1.9 million to 2.5 million** (itself noisy, with a step up in January 2026, so not to be read as a boom). So the survey's young lag the total but do not shrink. Each window rests on 369–521 survey responses from young developers, many from the same people in several months. The CPS's age bands (26–29, 30–34, 35–44, 45–54) cannot check the 41–49 rise. The Stanford authors say their own patterns are "more pronounced in the ADP analysis sample than in national survey benchmarks". **This is the evidence that most weakens the argument**: the strongest age evidence is from one private payroll provider's clients.
5. **Outside context: the explanations, attributed, with what this data can and cannot say.** One or two sentences each; the prose does not pick a winner.
   - **AI coding tools.** Brynjolfsson, Chandar and Chen (Stanford, August 2026) find young workers in AI-exposed occupations 19% below where they would be had they kept pace with less-exposed peers, robust to controls for interest-rate exposure, but call their results descriptive indicators "rather than causal estimates". Hosseini and Lichtinger (Harvard, May 2026) find junior employment falls at firms that adopt generative AI, through slower hiring. Against: Yale Budget Lab (October 2025) finds no "discernible disruption" in the broad labour market; Iscenko and Curto Millet (Economic Innovation Group, January 2026) find the slump in AI-exposed postings began in spring 2022. *This data:* half the postings fall predates ChatGPT; the young-employment gap opens after it. Consistent with AI playing a part in the second, not the first; not a test.
   - **Over-hiring and interest rates.** Iscenko and Curto Millet attribute the 2022 vacancy fall to "the sharpest monetary policy tightening cycle in four decades"; the Federal Reserve's first rise of the cycle was 16 March 2022. *This data:* the postings peak (late February 2022) and the fastest fall fit that timing, and software postings had more than doubled by the peak, which an over-hiring correction would unwind. All six countries' central banks also raised rates in 2022, so the six-country pattern is consistent with this too. Not tested.
   - **Section 174.** From tax years beginning in 2022, US firms had to spread software development costs over five years (15 for foreign R&D) rather than deduct them at once (Congressional Research Service); the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed 4 July 2025, restored immediate deduction for domestic R&D. Evidence it cut hiring is anecdotal (Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer). *This data:* no sign of a US-specific extra drop. The US software fall from peak (−66%) equals the UK's and Canada's and is smaller than Germany's (−71%), where the rule does not apply; and US software postings bottomed in the week of 17 May 2025, the same week as the UK's, before the repeal.
   - **Offshoring.** No systematic source found attributing the junior decline to it; a vendor sample (Jellyfish, January 2025) puts the offshore share of engineers at 49.7% in 2024, up from 39.9% in 2019. *This data:* cannot test it.
6. **How this was made.** Four snapshots with fetch scripts in `junior-dev-hiring-src/`; the CPS figures are aggregated from 80 monthly public-use microdata files by `fetch-cps.mjs`; charts by `junior-dev-hiring-make-charts.mjs`.

## Voice

Per the voice guide: plain; no "collapse of the career ladder", no "AI is eating junior jobs". State the difference, a fifth down against a fifth up, and let chart 4 keep it honest. The causes are a short list of attributed claims, each with one line on what the data can and cannot say. The author's voice pass is separate.

## Friction notes

- **Open mode needed a junior-vs-senior source that the obvious ones lack.** Indeed's open data has no seniority split; BLS tables by age refused automated download (HTTP 403); the Census API now needs a key. The age split came from the Stanford dashboard's public index (strong, but one provider's payroll clients) and CPS microdata aggregated here (public domain, too small). Neither alone would have been enough to write the wrinkle honestly.
- **The Census download server refuses some files.** The fixed-width files for October 2021 and January 2024 returned a "Request Rejected" HTML page with status 200 on every attempt; the CSV versions of the same months worked. `fetch-cps.mjs` checks the bytes rather than the status.
- **"Latest" URLs.** The Stanford ZIP lives at a `latest/` path that will change; the snapshot and manifest pin the vintage (17 September 2026).
- **A base month can flatter.** The source's own normalisation month (November 2022) sits on the youngest group's peak. The review caught it; the outline now gives the full-window change too.
