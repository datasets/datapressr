## Argument in one sentence

France's spending is dominated by social protection and health, with old age the largest social commitment; those commitments help explain the scale of the budget, but their recent evolution and the revenue record rule out a simple story of ever-expanding welfare causing the deficit.

### Revised argument and reader sequence

France's larger euro spending bill is led by retirement-related support and health; ageing contributes to pension pressure, but recent indexation and the revenue side also matter, and more euros need not mean a higher share of GDP.

1. Preserve the compact fiscal snapshot and overall spending mix. Define old age immediately beside the treemap as retirement income and support for older people, including retirement pensions, care allowances, accommodation, home help and administration. UN COFOG10.2.0 explicitly includes government-employee and military pension schemes. It is not a line for nursing homes alone; survivors and healthcare remain separate categories. Do not derive an unverified pensions/care pie from a different accounting framework.
2. Directly answer what is growing. New zero-based horizontal bars show changes in current €bn, with two vertically stacked panels for2014–2024 and2023–2024, both using mutually exclusive categories: GF1002 old age, GF10 minus GF1002 other social protection, and the other nine top-level functions. Sort each panel by its own change. All11 changes must add to TOTAL's change within0.002bn source rounding. Over2014–2024 total spending grows€414.1448bn; old age€99.1837bn, health€81.5841bn, other social protection€65.7591bn are the largest additions. In2023–2024 total grows€64.4013bn; old age€22.8755bn and health€11.4799bn increase while economic affairs falls€11.5475bn. Label each bar to one decimal €bn, retain a visible zero and negatives, and state the different panel scales (decade approximately−5 to110; year−15 to30). GDP ratios and real volumes are not being charted here. Never add GF10 parent on top of the two social-protection components.
3. Explain the denominator visually. Retain aggregate1995–2024 GDP-share history, but replace the subgroup comparison with two aligned panels: current-euro spending2014/2024 (common0–420€bn scale), then share of GDP2014/2024 (common0–15% scale). Same nine categories and row order, largest2024 euro amount first. Each panel has two explicit numeric columns labelled2014 and2024 in the same colours as the marks; values are not a single unexplained arrow. Both dots are filled, radius4.5;2014 orange #b65d16,2024 blue #2563eb. Small opposing vertical offsets keep coincident/nearby values visible; connector joins actual marker positions without shifting x values. Include actual coloured circle/year keys. Social protection€528.086→693.0288bn (+31.2341%) while GDP share24.5→23.7%; old age€292.7706→391.9543bn (+33.8776%) while13.6→13.4%GDP. These are current prices, not inflation-adjusted purchasing power. Label in large text: “More euros spent” and “Smaller/larger share of GDP” before the reader reaches the chart. Keep longer-run protection22.0→23.7%GDP and health6.9→8.9%GDP context; avoid implying that GDP-share declines mean nominal cuts.
4. Answer ageing with attributed evidence, not a new unsupported causal estimate. UN supplies the category definition. DREES2025 social-protection report, printed p36, describes baby-boom retirements and longer lives as increasing recipient numbers, with retirement reforms moderating expenditure dynamics. Printed pp54–57 identifies inflation-linked pension uprating as the main2024 pension-growth driver and rising recipients as another contributor: basic pension uprating5.3% from1January2024; CNAV recipient count+1.0% in2024. These two figures measure different populations/concepts and MUST NOT be added into a causal decomposition of the€392bn COFOG category. Retain as attributed external figures exempt from the chart-number rule, with a direct source link. Explain pension expenditure roughly as recipients × average payment, with eligibility/retirement age affecting the first and indexation/career mix affecting the second. No estimate of what fraction of the decade's COFOG increase was caused by ageing. DREES' broader protection accounts include some private schemes and differ from COFOG: use them for mechanisms, not substitution of totals.
5. Keep the deficit and debt context concise. Aim for no more than700 prose words, allowing the extra visual evidence to carry numerical detail. No new top-level dataset: the three validated CSVs already support growth calculations; new external context snapshots live in france-public-finances-src with provenance and hashes. Portable source/bundle includes this directory. Author voice pass remains outstanding.
