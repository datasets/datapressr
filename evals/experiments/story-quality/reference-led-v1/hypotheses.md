## R1 — Investigate a reader-relevant comparison before fixing the argument

**Location and change.** `skills/story/SKILL.md`, Step 0 after the existing `SUMMARY.md` paragraph. Treat an extreme as an investigative lead; select a question whose comparison helps the intended reader understand an expectation or everyday concern. Confirming an expectation remains useful when consequential. The small GDP example explicitly limits the inference to regional GDP per head, not household living standards.

**Trace.** `jbm-london` uses regional distributions and weighted exclusion to challenge a national aggregate ([thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1689950429403037697.html); assessed notes, “the comparison changes when its unit changes”). Its exact historical inputs and reported 14% remain unreproduced. Owner concern R1.5 says the France draft is “not very deep or compelling”; [the independent reader critique](../../../../docs/reviews/france-public-finances-reader-critique.md) finds a useful tension between spending scale and evolution. The diagnostic maps these to `interest`, `findings_depth` and `argument_counterevidence`.

**Before / after, constructed question adaptation.** “London has the highest GDP per head” becomes “How well does national GDP per head describe the country's regions?” The second question calls for evidence that can change the reader's interpretation of the headline, rather than another ranking. This is an original adaptation, not a claimed reproduction of the source's findings.

**Counterexample and limits.** A single record high can answer a commission without a subgroup investigation; no contrarian answer or universal disaggregation is required. Population weights, comparable regional boundaries and metric meaning must be established before reusing a quantitative exclusion. q02's capacity-versus-economic-size question is appealing, but its incompatible coalitions invalidate the comparison: interest cannot validate an answer. A fixed-data writer must work within supplied evidence, stating remaining limits rather than fetching forbidden sources.

**H1.** Relative to the baseline, candidate stories will more often let a masked reviewer state a specific reader payoff, identify the comparison supporting it, and explain how that comparison answers the commission. The reviewer writes that interpretation before seeing the outline or rule hypotheses, then verifies it against actual evidence. Code each output `supported-payoff`, `descriptive-only`, `unsupported-payoff` or `unassessable`, with passages/calculations. A question mark, promised usefulness, striking fact or named subgroup is insufficient. Report matched-pair direction and counts, with manual `interest`/`findings_depth` anchors as supporting evidence. Failure to improve supported interpretations, or improvement obtained through unsupported stakes, counts against H1. A well-supported confirmation can meet H1 without revising a prior belief.

## R2 — Choose the comparison before the chart form

**Location and change.** `skills/story/references/charting.md`, before the implementation pattern. Use the existing chart-plan purpose column to connect the intended comparison to its encoding: common positions for precise comparison, distribution plus aggregate for representativeness, separated outcomes for a trade-off. Read the chart before its explanatory prose to check whether that comparison is visible. No new review gate or chart-count requirement is added.

**Trace.** `jbm-rent-control` keeps covered-tenant benefit visible beside wider outcomes ([root post](https://bsky.app/profile/jburnmurdoch.ft.com/post/3mx2kxoezbk2x); assessed notes, “translate the evidence unit before the conclusion”). Separate outcome rows serve the “who gains and loses” question. They do not validate underlying result coding, which remains unresolved. Owner R1.3 asks for simple graphics that make comparisons easier. Hard counterexample: `q01-draft2-superficial-compliance` has labels and a year key but the owner still cannot decode the years or reconcile growth/share; historical critics praise the confusing passage. The target is `chart_design`, with G2/G4 assessed separately.

**Before / after, constructed chart adaptation.** Replace one pooled “beneficial versus harmful” verdict with outcome-separated comparisons that retain both the covered-tenant benefit and potential housing spillovers. This makes the trade-off recoverable. It is a hypothetical design contrast, not a claim that the source used the pooled design. A result-direction graphic can show mixed evidence; it cannot display effect magnitude or be numerically reconstructed from overlapping dots.

**Counterexample and limits.** Not every chart needs a distribution or multiple outcomes. A single line can directly answer a trend question. Precise comparison is one task; hierarchy, spatial pattern and other tasks may need other encodings. An informative chart must not change its whole-budget denominator, as the assessed spending 100% subset stack does. q02's deterministic but invalid legend remains a G4 failure even if its intended comparison is worthwhile.

**H2.** Relative to baseline, candidate charts will yield more correct chart-only interpretations of the intended comparison and fewer conflicts between first reading and precise reading. A masked reviewer records the conclusion, direction, unit/denominator and group/year identities before prose/outline, then records corrections and calculations needed after exact inspection. Code `comparison-recoverable`, `requires-reconstruction`, `misleading` or `unassessable` for each chart and explain why. Labels existing is not a pass; quoting a label without recovering the relation is reconstruction. Report output-level and matched-pair outcomes alongside chart counts, so extra charts do not create extra independent samples. Any actual owner reading is separately identified; this analyst exercise is not a timed reader experiment. No increase in recoverable comparisons, or more misleading first readings, counts against H2.

## R3 — Explain the relationship between findings

**Location and change.** `skills/story/references/voice-guide.md`, after “The one rule”, with one synthetic before/after. Interpret how the next finding qualifies or helps explain the previous one, within the approved evidence. Numerical concision should not produce disconnected statistics; interpretation must preserve the distinction between observation and mechanism.

**Trace.** Owner R2.5 asks how social protection could grow while nearly every area shrank; the manual diagnosis identifies an actual comprehension failure despite present nominal/share wording. R2.3 separately asks what old age means and whether ageing explains it. Neither is solved by another generic caveat. `bostock-example` ([For Example](https://bost.ocks.org/mike/example/)) supports a bounded explanatory sequence connecting a concrete example, purpose and consequence; it is practitioner explanation, not a verified journalism benchmark or tested historical interaction. Targets: `explanation`, `writing`, and applicable `reader_coverage`.

**Before / after, explicitly synthetic.** Before: “Spending rose from £100 to £120. Its share of the economy fell from 10% to 8%.” After: “Spending rose from £100 to £120, but the economy grew faster, from £1,000 to £1,500. That larger denominator means spending increased in pounds while falling from 10% to 8% of the economy.” The arithmetic is £100/£1,000 = 10%, £120/£1,500 = 8%; spending rises 20%, the economy 50%. These are constructed figures, not France observations. The added relationship lets a reader reconcile both statements. It does not establish causation or real growth after inflation.

**Counterexample and limits.** A simple self-contained finding needs no manufactured transition. An external causal explanation still needs named, permitted, verified attribution; a spending share cannot identify demographic or pension-policy effects. q03's attributed storage/expiry explanation should not be erased merely because price data cannot identify its mechanism. Its missing computed spread is already covered by baseline story-craft guidance and is not claimed as a new R3 rule.

**H3.** Relative to baseline, candidate prose will better support a masked reviewer's account of how material findings fit together, what they imply, and what they do not establish. For each output, record passage-located relationships and the reviewer's interpretation before consulting the outline. After evidence checking, code `supported-link-understood`, `link-left-to-reader`, `unsupported-link` or `unassessable`. For an apparent amount/share contradiction, a successful interpretation must explain the changing denominator, not simply repeat both percentages; for a claimed mechanism it must distinguish attribution from local identification. Report relationship counts within outputs and matched-pair outcomes, with `explanation`/`writing` anchors; connective-word counts and shorter prose are not success metrics. No comprehension gain, or added causal overreach, counts against H3.

## Reconciliation with existing guidance

Baseline story-craft already requires reader-question lists, largest-category drill-down, meaningful differences/ratios, growth denominators, counterevidence and attributed mechanisms. It separates composition from change, discourages chart narration and recommends comparative bars. Charting already covers direct labels, appropriate references, units, gaps, deterministic builds and delivered-size inspection. Editorial review already checks interest and reader questions independently. Those files/rules are preserved. R1 changes how a question is selected; R2 connects the reader's comparative task to chart design; R3 explains relationships rather than adding more figures or caveats. Installed guidance contains no required reference to this repository's research or evaluation files.
