## Strongest findings missed

- The outline reports that the deficit narrowed from 5.8% of GDP in 2024 to 5.1% in 2025, an important counterweight to an account of worsening urgency.
- The outline’s 2017 comparison shows revenue falling from 54.3% to 52.1% of GDP while spending fell from 57.7% to 57.2%, making the recent fiscal deterioration look different from the selected 2019 baseline.
- The outline places 2025 interest payments of 2.2% of GDP below the 3.5–3.6% recorded in 1995–1997, useful context for distinguishing rising pressure from an unprecedented burden.
- The supplied 2025 spending and revenue figures imply an EU27 deficit of 3.1% of GDP, about two percentage points smaller than France’s, a more relevant comparison than the size of government alone.
- A diagnosis of an immediate financing crisis requires evidence on market borrowing costs, refinancing needs and access to funding, none of which is supplied.
- The story leaves out the debt-stabilising fiscal adjustment, its political feasibility and who would bear its costs.

## Lessons

1. Answer the commissioned question explicitly; a transparent limitation does not substitute for the missing answer. Evidence: The story explains persistent deficits but only near the end admits it cannot assess the claimed crisis.
2. Separate accounting identities from explanations of economic causes. Evidence: Spending exceeding revenue explains borrowing, but does not establish the respective roles of policy choices, weak growth and shocks.
3. Test a recent-change argument against more than one defensible starting year. Evidence: The outline’s 2017 baseline shows falling spending and revenue shares, whereas the prose’s 2019 baseline shows spending rising.
4. Distinguish growth in a debt stock from growth in its ratio to GDP, and substantiate the denominator’s drivers. Evidence: The story records a falling debt ratio alongside rising euro debt, but attributes the change to inflation without showing the real-growth and price contributions.
5. Give charts a visible conclusion as well as labels and units. Evidence: The debt and spending charts label selected observations clearly but leave the reader to formulate their central comparisons.
