---
title: "EIA, Today in Energy, 27 April 2020: Low liquidity and limited available storage pushed WTI crude oil futures prices below zero"
---

Key findings of a published reference piece, as recorded by us. The original's prose and charts are not reproduced. Source: https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=43495

- On Monday 20 April 2020 the front-month (May 2020) WTI futures contract on NYMEX traded below zero for the first time since WTI futures began trading in 1983, as low as about -$40 a barrel intraday, and stayed below zero for part of the next trading day.
- The cause given is the timing of the May contract's expiry in a collapsing market: holders still long at expiry must take physical delivery at Cushing, Oklahoma (only about 1% of futures go to delivery), and with no buyers and no storage some sold at negative prices, in effect paying others to take the oil.
- Demand had collapsed under COVID-19 travel restrictions and the economic slowdown: US refinery runs in the week to 17 April were 12.8 million barrels a day, 4.1 million (24%) below a year earlier.
- Storage at Cushing was scarce: about 58 million barrels in tank farms against 76 million barrels of working capacity on 17 April (76% full), and much of the rest likely already leased or committed, so uncommitted space was limited and expensive.
- The negative price was largely a feature of the expiring financial contract: longer-dated WTI contracts and most other US spot crudes stayed positive, and Brent for June delivery closed at $19.33 on 21 April.
- EIA expected Cushing storage constraints to keep causing volatility in the June WTI contract and in spot prices for crudes with limited storage options.
