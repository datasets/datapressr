# Reader A: chart-first impressions

These notes were composed after reading the commission and instrument and viewing all four assigned PNGs, before reading the assigned story prose. The first attempt to persist this exact draft failed because `python` was unavailable; prose was displayed in the following operation before that error was noticed. This file was consequently saved using `python3` after prose exposure. This is a fresh retrospective reading, not a holdout or perfectly blind assessment. No prior story, author, arm, or rating material was inspected.

- Chart 1: The first impression is a ratchet upward in French debt, with sharp jumps around the financial crisis and pandemic, a post-pandemic dip, and a renewed rise to 119.0% in mid-2026. Direct endpoint/event labels make the history easy to read. The cause of the renewed rise is not answered by this chart alone.
- Chart 2: Persistent deficits, including deficits before interest since 2002, emerge clearly. The grey/red/blue decomposition needs a moment of decoding: in primary-surplus years, blue rises above zero while the black total marker lies within grey. The 2025 right-hand annotation is dense and wraps into four lines. Labels are legible in the tool rendering I saw; I did not observe a collision or clipping.
- Chart 3: France has both the highest revenue and highest spending among the displayed comparators, and its gap is largest. Direct numerical labels and explicit truncated-axis note help. The headline “collects the most” is broader than the visible, selected-country comparison, and the adjacent deficit column uses positive magnitudes while the before-interest column uses signed balances, requiring a small interpretation step.
- Chart 4: Old-age benefits and health rose while general public services and several other functions fell. The old-age subcomponent and debt-interest annotations are useful. The two-endpoint design shows composition change, but cannot establish the timing or causes of it. No visible collision or clipping stood out.

Viewing: each PNG was opened with view_image(detail="original"). Intrinsic dimensions and intended display width are supplied by the packet, but the actual on-screen display dimensions/viewport are not exposed; I cannot certify readability at the intended 720px width from this viewing.
