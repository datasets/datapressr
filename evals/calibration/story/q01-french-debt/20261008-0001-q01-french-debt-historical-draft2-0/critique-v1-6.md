## Strongest findings missed

- The central distinction between a persistent fiscal imbalance and an immediate debt crisis requires evidence about financing costs, market access or political capacity that the story never provides.
- The debt ratio rose 9.5 percentage points between end-2023 and June 2026 despite the reported 2025 deficit improvement, making the relationship between smaller deficits and continuing debt growth a stronger explanatory focus.
- The 2025 improvement came from revenue rising 0.9 percentage points of GDP while spending rose 0.2 points, a useful counterpart to the earlier revenue-led deterioration.
- An explicit interest bill, comparable-country benchmarks and a repayment profile are needed to assess whether France’s borrowing burden is unusually dangerous.
- A debt-stabilisation calculation with stated growth and interest assumptions, alongside political constraints and the distribution of adjustment costs, would connect the accounting history to the choices France faces.

## Lessons

1. Answer the commissioned question before expanding into a related investigation. Evidence: The commission asks why there is a debt crisis; the title and opening instead ask where public money goes.
2. Distinguish evidence of high indebtedness from evidence of a financing crisis. Evidence: The story supplies a 119% debt ratio but no borrowing-cost, repayment or market-access evidence.
3. Connect fiscal flows to debt dynamics with an explicit stabilisation benchmark. Evidence: The deficit improves in 2025 while debt subsequently reaches 119% of GDP, but the reader is not shown what balance would stabilise the ratio.
4. Use counter-evidence to test simple explanations. Evidence: Falling social-protection spending relative to GDP, revenue weakness and the 2025 improvement usefully challenge an account based solely on expanding welfare.
5. Give each chart one main analytical task. Evidence: The evolution figure combines aggregate history, nine subgroup comparisons and nominal-growth attribution, diluting its otherwise clear headline.
